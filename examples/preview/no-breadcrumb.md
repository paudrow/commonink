title: The heading names the note
---
The breadcrumb and "‹ Notes" are gone from the top bar. The ← → arrows take you back, and a note's heading is its name.

1. Open [[Draft to rename]]. The top bar starts with ← →, with no "‹ Notes" and no folder or name. Hover the Move button (the folder with an arrow). It says "In Try / The heading names the note · Move to another folder". Open [[Filed away]] and hover it again: the path has one more folder.
2. Back in [[Draft to rename]], change its heading to `# Renamed by its heading` and click in the text below. About a second and a half later the address and the tab title change, and a toast says it updated the links to it. Typing on the heading line and waiting doesn't rename it. Moving off the line, clicking away or opening something else does.
3. Open [[Links to the draft]]. Its link now reads `[[Renamed by its heading]]` and still opens the note.
4. Open [[No heading yet]], press ⌘⇧P (Ctrl+Shift+P off a Mac) and pick **Rename note…**. A `# No heading yet` line appears at the top with the name selected. Type a new name and press ↓. The note is renamed.
5. Open [[Titled by its properties]] and run **Rename note…**. Its title comes from its `title:` property, so a small dialog asks for the name instead. HTML notes, like Dashboards/Reading stats, work the same way.
6. Split the view (⌘⌥\, or ⌘-click a link). Each pane's bar has ← →, ☆ and × only. Open Notes, Tasks or History: the top bar has no page name, since each page has its own heading. From a note, open its History (the clock button). The note it's narrowed to shows as a chip.
7. Narrow the window below 760px. The bar is ☰, ←, ☆, search and ⋯, and ⋯'s Move item says which folder the note is in.
